An interactive exhibit · Auckland, 2026

What happens when a city starts charging drivers?

Auckland is planning to charge cars that enter the city centre in the morning rush. Nobody knows exactly how 19,000 commuters will react. So we built a small city of simulated commuters, gave them a charge, and watched. Then we did it again with an AI deciding for each of them.

Everything here is real and running The maps replay the actual simulation, minute by minute. The numbers are recomputed from the run files. And on the Try it yourself page the full model runs inside your browser, no installation needed.

The big idea: people don't decide every day

Think about your own commute. You don't weigh up every option each morning; you do what you did yesterday. You only stop and rethink when something changes: a new charge appears, you keep arriving late, the train is cancelled.

Most traffic models forget this. They make every simulated person decide afresh every day with a formula. This model gives each commuter a cognitive clock: an alarm that only goes off when something worth thinking about happens. The rest of the time, habit runs the show. That is what makes it cheap enough to put an AI inside: on an ordinary morning, nobody needs to think at all.

  1. Look at the options Each commuter can drive (leaving at one of several times), take the bus or train, or, if their job allows, work from home. For each option they know roughly how long it takes, what it costs and when they'd arrive.
  2. Does the alarm go off? Six things can wake a commuter: it's the first day, the charge changed, they were late yesterday, their train was disrupted, the drive was much slower than expected, or their usual choice is no longer possible.
  3. Decide, if woken Either a formula picks the cheapest option (counting time as money), or an AI reads a plain-language description of the person and situation and answers like that person would, giving a reason.
  4. Drive the morning Cars travel along real Auckland roads, slow down where traffic is heavy, queue at the gate, and arrive. Bus and train trips take a fixed time.
  5. Remember How long it took, how late they were, what they paid. Tomorrow's alarm and tomorrow's options use this memory.

Same city, same charge, two different stories

The formula-driven commuters mostly kept driving but left earlier to dodge the peak price, creating a brand-new traffic peak before 7:30. The AI-driven commuters mostly switched to the bus or train and kept their usual departure time. Both reactions cleared the roads by about the same amount for those who kept driving.

Neither is “the truth”. The formula was tuned to match what happened in Stockholm when it introduced a charge; the AI was not tuned at all. The point of the exhibit is to see how two different kinds of decision-maker reach their answers, and what a city should prepare for either way. See What we found.
